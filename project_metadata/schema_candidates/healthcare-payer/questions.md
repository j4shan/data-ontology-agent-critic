# Harbor Medicare Services

These questions are analytical decisions for ontology assessment. Each asks for a comparison, concentration, gap, trend, or exception across a population.

## Medical economics analyst

This role owns how condition capture and stored risk scores change expected revenue by plan and member cohort. A claim diagnosis, the category that diagnosis maps to, a diagnosis captured for risk adjustment, and the month-end risk score are different populations. Answering the questions requires choosing those populations and the member attributes that define a cohort, rather than replaying one fixed operational query.

| request_desc | impact | question | core_datasets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compare, by plan and member cohort, the rate at which claim diagnoses map to a condition category that was never captured for risk adjustment. | Missing this gap understates expected revenue for the plans and cohorts where reported conditions never reached risk adjustment. | Which plans and member cohorts have the highest rate of members with a claim diagnosis that maps to a hierarchical condition category but no captured risk-adjustment diagnosis for that category? | member; plan; coverage span; line of business; claim header; claim diagnosis; diagnosis; hierarchical condition category; captured risk diagnosis; risk model |
| Locate where elevated month-end risk scores concentrate across plans and demographic cohorts. | Sending the revenue forecast to the wrong plans misstates expected revenue for the book. | Where do elevated month-end risk scores concentrate across plans, lines of business, and member cohorts defined by gender, state, and jurisdiction? | member; gender; state; jurisdiction; plan; line of business; coverage span; risk score; risk model |
| Compare the mix of categories captured for risk adjustment with the mix implied by diagnoses on claims. | Treating the stored score as complete when those mixes diverge misstates expected revenue by plan. | For which plans does the mix of hierarchical condition categories captured for risk adjustment diverge most from the categories implied by diagnoses reported on claims? | member; plan; claim header; claim diagnosis; diagnosis; hierarchical condition category; captured risk diagnosis; risk score; risk model |
| Find risk models and plans where captured risk diagnoses do not map to a condition category. | Counting unmapped captures as if they raised the score overstates expected revenue for those plans. | Which risk models and plans have the highest share of captured risk-adjustment diagnoses that map to no hierarchical condition category? | member; plan; captured risk diagnosis; diagnosis; hierarchical condition category; risk model; risk score |
| Compare the risk-score profile of members who disenroll with the profile of members who remain. | If higher scores are leaving a plan, expected revenue falls even when that plan's premium schedule is unchanged. | Which plans concentrate disenrollments among members whose month-end risk scores are higher than the scores of members who remain enrolled? | member; plan; coverage span; enrollment span; disenrollment; risk score; risk model; premium schedule |

## Utilization management lead

An authorization, its requested lines, and its single decision are separate from an appeal on a claim, and no stored link ties an authorization to a later claim. Answering the questions requires choosing those populations and a path through member, provider, procedure, and plan, rather than replaying one fixed operational query.
