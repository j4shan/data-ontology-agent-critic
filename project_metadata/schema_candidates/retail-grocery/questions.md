# Greenbasket Markets

Catalog: `retail-grocery`

These questions are analytical decisions for ontology assessment. Each asks for a comparison, concentration, gap, trend, or exception across a population, and each one is answered by choosing the populations and the path that connect them.

## Category merchant

The category merchant decides which merchandise categories keep a promotion, a price, and a place in the assortment once sales, discounts, and shrink are read together. Answering that requires choosing the category population and a path through items, basket lines, promotions, prices, costs, markdowns, and shrink, rather than replaying one fixed operational query for a single item.

| request_desc | impact | question | core_datasets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compare each merchandise category's selling rate before a promotion with its selling rate after the promotion ends. | Renewing a promotion for a category that does not hold its sales spends margin the category will not earn back. | Which merchandise categories sell a smaller share of their quantity after a promotion ends than they sold before it began, compared with categories in the same department that were not promoted? | merchandise category; merchandise department; item; promotion; basket line; calendar day |
| Measure which categories give up the largest share of sales to line discounts and shrink together. | A category treated as healthy on shelf price will keep receiving offers after discounts and shrink have already consumed the sale. | In which merchandise categories do line discounts and shrink events together account for a larger share of sales than in the other categories of the same department? | merchandise category; merchandise department; item; basket line; discount; shrink event |
| Find price zones where a category's retail price falls behind its cost as promoted lines become a larger part of the mix. | A price file that no longer covers cost is shared by every storefront in the zone. | Which merchandise categories in which price zones show a widening gap between unit cost and retail price while a rising share of basket lines cite a promotion? | merchandise category; item; price zone; retail price; item cost; basket line; promotion |
| Identify categories whose markdowns and shrink cluster in the same storefronts. | Markdown money and shrink are the same items leaving at two valuations, and splitting them hides the storefronts where both concentrate. | Which merchandise categories concentrate markdowns and shrink in the same storefronts at a higher rate than that category shows across the banner? | merchandise category; item; storefront; banner; markdown; shrink event; shrink reason |
| Find categories whose returns after a promotion offset more of the promoted sales than peer categories. | Promotional volume that comes back as returns will be planned again as if it were demand. | Which merchandise categories are exceptions whose customer returns, after a promotion, offset a larger share of promoted basket lines than other categories in the same department? | merchandise category; merchandise department; item; promotion; basket line; customer return; calendar day |

## Store operations lead

The store operations lead decides which storefronts need labor, a waste review, or an in-stock recovery when empty shelves, discarded product, and thin coverage pile up together. Answering that requires choosing storefronts and a path across out-of-stock observations, waste, schedules, punches, and drawer sessions, rather than replaying one fixed operational query for a single shift.

| request_desc | impact | question | core_datasets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Find open storefronts where out-of-stock observations, waste, and scheduled shifts without a labor punch fall on the same days. | Staffing to the schedule while the shelf is empty and product is discarded spends labor on a storefront that is already losing the sale. | Which open storefronts concentrate out-of-stock observations, waste events, and scheduled shifts without a matching labor punch on the same days, at a higher rate than other storefronts in the district? | storefront; district; out-of-stock observation; waste event; scheduled shift; labor punch; calendar day |
| Compare storefronts where an item is wasted and later recorded out of stock on days with thin labor coverage. | Replenishing a storefront that discards the item, without coverage to work the shelf, repeats the loss. | Which storefronts show the widest gap between waste of an item and a later out-of-stock observation for that item, on days when labor punches cover the smallest share of scheduled shifts? | storefront; item; waste event; out-of-stock observation; scheduled shift; labor punch; calendar day |
| Within each store format, locate days when out-of-stock observations and waste coincide with the labor roles that are scheduled most and punched least. | Adding hours on the wrong labor role leaves the empty-shelf days uncovered. | Within each store format, which storefronts concentrate out-of-stock observations and waste on days when the labor roles with the most scheduled shifts are the roles least often punched? | storefront; store format; out-of-stock observation; waste event; labor role; scheduled shift; labor punch; calendar day |
| Track storefronts whose share of district waste and out-of-stock observations rises while cashier drawer sessions do not. | A storefront losing product and availability without more till coverage will be managed as if the problem were only sales. | Which storefronts show a rising share of their district's waste events and out-of-stock observations over time while cashier drawer sessions stay flat or decline? | storefront; district; waste event; out-of-stock observation; cashier; drawer session; calendar day |
| Surface storefronts that are regional exceptions on in-stock service, waste, and whether scheduled labor roles were punched. | A region score hides the few storefronts where empty shelves, discards, and missed roles sit together. | Which storefronts are exceptions in their region, with a weaker in-stock service level, a higher waste rate, and a lower share of scheduled labor roles that were punched? | storefront; region; service level; waste event; labor role; scheduled shift; labor punch |

## Replenishment analyst

The replenishment analyst decides which distribution centers and vendors are missing storefront demand, and whether the miss sits in the vendor fill, the shipment, or the store receipt. Answering that requires choosing those populations and a path through forecasts, replenishment orders, purchase orders, shipments, and receipts, rather than replaying one fixed operational query for a single shipment.

| request_desc | impact | question | core_datasets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compare each distribution center's shipments, and the receipts at the storefronts it serves, with demand forecasts over time. | A distribution center whose gap is widening will miss the same storefronts on the next order cycle. | Which distribution centers show a widening gap between storefront demand forecasts and the quantities shipped to those storefronts, and between those shipments and the quantities the storefronts receive? | distribution center; storefront; item; demand forecast; distribution-center shipment; shipment line; store receipt; receipt line; calendar day |
| Find vendors that fill a low share of ordered quantity on items storefronts are still trying to replenish. | Pressing the distribution center for an item the vendor did not fill delays the shortage that can actually be recovered. | Which vendors concentrate short fills on purchase orders for items whose replenishment orders stay large relative to what distribution centers then ship to the requesting storefronts? | vendor; vendor site; purchase order; purchase-order line; vendor fill; item; replenishment order; distribution center; distribution-center shipment; shipment line; storefront |
| Measure where quantities received at storefronts fall short of quantities shipped, as a share of forecast demand. | A receipt gap concentrated at one distribution center is a shipping or receiving failure, and ordering more into that path repeats it. | For which distribution centers is the gap between quantity shipped to storefronts and quantity those storefronts receive the largest share of forecast demand? | distribution center; storefront; item; shipment line; receipt line; demand forecast |
| Identify primary distribution centers whose storefronts go out of stock while receipts lag both the forecast and the replenishment order. | The recovery is a distribution-center decision, and a single storefront count will not show which center is the exception. | Which primary distribution centers are exceptions, serving storefronts whose out-of-stock observations rise while received quantities stay below both the demand forecast and the replenishment order? | distribution center; storefront; item; out-of-stock observation; demand forecast; replenishment order; store receipt; receipt line |
| Compare what each vendor site fills with what distribution centers later ship for the same items to storefronts that asked for them. | A site that fills the purchase order while the storefront still misses demand points the recovery at the distribution center, and the reverse points it at the vendor. | Which vendor sites show the widest gap between filled purchase-order quantity and the quantity distribution centers ship to storefronts that placed replenishment orders for the same items? | vendor site; vendor; purchase-order line; vendor fill; item; distribution center; shipment line; storefront; replenishment order |

## Loyalty analyst

The loyalty analyst decides which households the program is retaining when trip frequency and point redemption move apart from the rest of the program. Answering that requires choosing households and a path through shoppers, loyalty accounts, visits, baskets, and redemptions, rather than replaying one fixed operational query for a single basket.

| request_desc | impact | question | core_datasets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compare household trip frequency with the rate of point redemption inside a loyalty program. | A reward aimed at frequent households misses the ones who visit and rarely redeem, and over-serves households who redeem without the trips. | Which households visit more often than other households in the same loyalty program, yet redeem points at a lower rate than households with a similar visit frequency? | household; customer; loyalty program; loyalty account; household visit; point redemption |
| Find households whose point redemptions concentrate in one selling channel while their visits follow the program's usual channel mix. | An offer built for the channel where points are redeemed misses the channels where the household actually shops. | Which households concentrate point redemptions on baskets in a single selling channel, while their visits stay spread across channels in line with the rest of the loyalty program? | household; customer; loyalty account; point redemption; basket; channel; household visit; loyalty program |
| Track households whose visit frequency falls while their point redemption rises, against the rest of the program. | Households redeeming points as their visits fall are leaving, and a frequency reward reads their redemption as engagement. | Which households show a falling visit rate and a rising point-redemption rate over time, compared with other households in the same loyalty program? | household; household visit; calendar day; customer; loyalty account; point redemption; loyalty program |
| Find households whose trip frequency and point redemption split from the pattern of the customer segment their shoppers belong to. | A segment offer assumes the segment shops and redeems together, and the exception households make that offer look right for the wrong households. | Which households are exceptions among households whose shoppers share a customer segment, with visit frequency and point redemption moving in opposite directions from that segment? | household; customer; customer segment; household visit; loyalty account; point redemption |
| See which districts concentrate visits from households that redeem heavily while visiting less often than the program. | Redemption cost sitting in districts where infrequent households still visit changes which storefronts the program should treat as loyalty stores. | Which districts concentrate visits from households that redeem points at a high rate while visiting less often than the rest of the loyalty program? | district; storefront; household; household visit; customer; loyalty account; point redemption; loyalty program |

## Space planner

The space planner decides which storefronts to reset when a planogram fails for a category customers are still buying. Answering that requires choosing storefronts and a path from category through subcategory, planogram, compliance, fixtures, assortment, and sales, rather than replaying one fixed operational query for a single compliance check.

| request_desc | impact | question | core_datasets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compare planogram compliance by storefront with the sales share of the subcategory the planogram presents. | Resetting a failed planogram on a subcategory that still sells either protects a selling set or spends the reset on a set the store has dropped. | Which storefronts fail planogram compliance for subcategories that still contribute a large share of their merchandise category's sales in the district? | storefront; district; planogram; planogram compliance; subcategory; merchandise category; item; basket line |
| Find storefronts where failed compliance checks sit on fixtures whose planned items are still selling. | Replanning a fixture from a blank wipes a presentation customers are already buying. | Which storefronts concentrate failed planogram checks on fixtures whose planned items still sell at that storefront, compared with fixtures that pass the same planogram? | storefront; fixture; aisle; planogram; planogram position; planogram compliance; item; basket line |
| Track storefronts where planogram compliance for a category worsens while that category's sales hold or rise. | Cutting a category for weak compliance removes demand the storefront is still taking. | Which storefronts show planogram compliance getting worse over time for a merchandise category whose sales at those storefronts are flat or rising? | storefront; merchandise category; subcategory; planogram; planogram compliance; item; basket line; calendar day |
| Compare storefronts that fail a planogram with storefronts that pass it, for items that remain in the assortment and continue to sell. | Delisting an item that is out of plan but still selling treats a presentation failure as a demand failure. | Which storefronts fail compliance on a planogram while other storefronts pass that same planogram, for items that remain in the assortment and continue to sell at the failing storefronts? | storefront; planogram; planogram compliance; planogram position; assortment; item; subcategory; basket line |
| See which districts concentrate planogram failures inside categories that still produce an outsized share of banner sales. | A banner-wide reset misses the districts where a selling category is the one out of plan. | Which districts concentrate planogram failures in merchandise categories that still account for a disproportionate share of sales across the banner? | district; storefront; banner; merchandise category; subcategory; planogram; planogram compliance; basket line |

## Fresh production lead

The fresh production lead decides which storefronts and recipes to cut back or remake when batches, waste, and sales of the finished item diverge. Answering that requires choosing those populations and a path through recipes, ingredients, production batches, waste, and sales, rather than replaying one fixed operational query for a single batch.

| request_desc | impact | question | core_datasets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compare production-batch quantity with sales and same-day waste of the finished item at each storefront. | Producing ahead of sales and then discarding the finished item sets the next batch from a recipe the storefront is already wasting. | Which storefronts produce a recipe well above sales of its finished item and also record waste of that item on the same days, compared with other storefronts making that recipe? | storefront; recipe; production batch; finished item; basket line; waste event; calendar day |
| Rank recipes by how much of their production is wasted rather than sold, within a district. | A recipe whose waste share is high in one district and low in another is a store execution problem, and dropping it banner-wide removes sales other storefronts are keeping. | Which recipes at which storefronts show waste taking a larger share of production than sales of the finished item, relative to the same recipe in the rest of the district? | recipe; storefront; district; production batch; finished item; waste event; basket line |
| Find storefronts where finished-item sales run ahead of recorded production while ingredient waste is high. | Selling more than the storefront recorded as produced, while ingredients are discarded, means batches are missing or the recipe is being broken before it is finished. | Which storefronts show sales of a finished item running ahead of recorded production for its recipe, together with a higher waste rate on that recipe's ingredients than storefronts where production covers sales? | storefront; recipe; ingredient; production batch; finished item; basket line; waste event |
| Track storefront and recipe pairs whose production and waste move together while finished-item sales move the other way. | Raising the batch because production is up adds waste at storefronts where customers are buying less of the finished item. | Over time, which storefronts and recipes show production and waste of the finished item moving in the same direction while sales of that item move in the other direction? | storefront; recipe; production batch; finished item; waste event; basket line; calendar day |
| Identify recipes whose ingredients are wasted at storefronts where the finished item still sells through. | Cutting a recipe that still sells, because ingredients are wasted, removes a finished item other storefronts produce with less ingredient waste. | Which recipes are exceptions, with high ingredient waste at storefronts where the finished item still sells, compared with storefronts that produce the same recipe and waste less of its ingredients? | recipe; ingredient; storefront; waste event; production batch; finished item; basket line |
