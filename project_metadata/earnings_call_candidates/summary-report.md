## What “rich BI dependencies” means here

A high question count is not enough. A useful ontology experiment is a pair whose questions
repeatedly join several of these objects: product or service, customer or channel, geography,
supply or capacity, and time. Pairs were scored by combined question count and by how often a
question names three or more objects and states an explicit comparison.

## Recommended experiments

Start with these six pairs. Each one gives both companies a four-call sheet, and the questions
share an entity chain rather than a single KPI.

1. **Health care equipment — Stryker (74) and Intuitive Surgical (31).** The densest clinical
   operating chain in the pool: procedure volume, capital equipment, product franchise, hospital
   customer, reimbursement, backlog, and supply disruption. Stryker’s sheet is the single largest.
   This is a different graph from the existing healthcare-payer catalog, which is claims,
   eligibility, and remittance.

2. **Automobile manufacturers — General Motors (65) and Tesla (30).** Highest share of questions
   that name three or more objects. The recurring joins are vehicle line, software subscription
   on the installed base, plant changeover, commodity cost, dealer inventory, and region.
   General Motors is the better first company file; Tesla covers the same chain with fewer asks.

3. **Technology hardware — Apple (50) and Dell (39).** Analysts keep separating demand, supply,
   and price: memory and silicon constraints, device family, services attach, channel, and
   geography. The two companies sit on the same component chain from different positions
   (device OEM and server/PC OEM), which is a good test of a shared supply graph.

4. **Restaurants — McDonald’s (47) and Starbucks (45).** The most balanced pair. Questions
   split traffic from check, company stores from franchisees, daypart and geography from
   commodity cost. The entity set is small enough to simulate without a conglomerate catalog.

5. **Data center REITs — Equinix (44) and Digital Realty (40).** Occupancy, power, interconnection,
   customer vertical, and metro recur on both sheets. Narrower than a bank or an oil major, so
   this is the cleanest bounded graph if the first simulation should stay small.

6. **Construction machinery — Caterpillar (39) and PACCAR (65).** Dealer inventory, end-market
   mix, price versus volume, and backlog. This is the industrial pair to try before the broader
   specialty-chemicals or airline sheets, which are large but more often a price-volume-region
   triangle than a multi-entity product graph.

## Pairs to defer

- **Specialty chemicals (113), managed care (106), airlines (104), and packaged foods (96)**
  have the largest counts. Managed care overlaps the existing payer assessment domain. The
  others are mostly price, volume, and geography, with fewer distinct entities to join.
- **Application software (43) and property & casualty insurance (44)** are the thinnest sheets.
  Salesforce kept only 19 questions across four calls, and two of those calls came from the
  fallback source.
- **Payments (71)** is consistent across Visa and Mastercard, but the asks cluster on volume,
  yield, and geography rather than a product-customer-supply chain.
