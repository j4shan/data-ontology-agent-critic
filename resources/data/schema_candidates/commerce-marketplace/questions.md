# Meridian Marketplace

Catalog: `commerce-marketplace`

These questions are analytical decisions for ontology assessment. Each asks for a comparison, concentration, gap, trend, or exception across sellers, buyers, listings, or categories, and each one is answered by aggregating the named populations.

## Seller economics manager

The seller economics manager decides which sellers are commercially sound: whether settlement proceeds, commissions, fees, refunds, and chargebacks move with the value of that seller's orders, and whether the seller tier still fits. Answering requires choosing the seller population and the separate money populations that explain it. A settlement line, a fee assessment, a refund, and a chargeback are different documents, and no single payout shows which sellers diverge.

## Trust and safety analyst

The trust and safety analyst decides where fraud decisions and listing suppressions concentrate, and whether the same sellers, listings, checkouts, or payment providers drive both. Answering requires choosing those populations and the seller, buyer, channel, and rule populations around them. A fraud decision may cite an order, a checkout, a fraud rule, or none of them, and a suppression cites a listing and a reason code.

## Fulfillment analyst

The fulfillment analyst decides which sellers, warehouses, and carriers concentrate backorders, unsourced allocations, thin inventory, and shipments that never complete, including cross-border declarations. Answering requires choosing the order line population and the allocation, backorder, inventory, shipment, and delivery populations around it. Unmet demand, a reservation, and a shipment are separate documents, and a warehouse is present on only some of them.

Answering requires choosing the price list population, the price change population, and the listing population. Contract prices and quotes belong to negotiated buying. A consumer order can be read apart from those agreements because an order may cite no contract and a buyer account records whether it is a business or a consumer.

The contrast is available because an order may cite a contract, a quote is a separate document, a listing is the published offer, and a buyer account records whether it is a business or a consumer.
